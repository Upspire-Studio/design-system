# Voice and writing

The family sounds like a capable friend who has run a nonprofit: plain, warm, specific, never salesy.

## Principles

- **Plain words.** Write the way you'd say it across a table. "Find where the right nonprofit jobs get posted" beats "Discover curated opportunities".
- **Name the real problem.** Volunteer Shift Manager names the pains people already live with: "The Spreadsheet Shuffle", "The Group Text Spiral", "The Last-Minute Scramble", "The Enterprise Price Tag".
- **Respect the reader's time and budget.** Say what's free, plainly: "It's free, and there's no account to set up."
- **Specific over clever.** Use real numbers and nouns: "18 board members", "300+ job boards", "Saturday 9:00–12:00".
- **Encourage, don't lecture.** "Here's how to do it well, step by step" (Nonprofit Roles, on hiring an executive director).

## Mechanics

- Sentence case for every heading, button and label. Only `label` eyebrows are uppercase, and that's styling, not typing.
- "You" for the reader, "we" for the product team. Never "users".
- Buttons are verbs that say what happens: "Publish schedule", "Send reminder", "Join the waitlist".
- Errors say what went wrong and how to fix it, with no apology and no blame: "Row 12 is missing an email address. Fix the file and upload it again."
- Empty states say what will appear here and how to add the first one.
- No emoji, no exclamation marks in UI, no "simply" or "just".
- Numbers as numerals. Times as "9:00–12:00". Dates as "Sat, Oct 11".

## Words

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| volunteers, board members, donors | users, resources, leads |
| nonprofit, organization | NPO, client |
| shift, schedule | slot, roster entry |
| keep donors giving | maximize donor LTV |
| free | freemium, at no cost to you |
