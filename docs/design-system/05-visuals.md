# Visuals and imagery

## Photography

- Real people doing real nonprofit work: sorting food, meeting around a table, mentoring, planting. Natural light, candid moments, warm color grading that sits well on `paper`.
- Show the diversity of the sector honestly: ages, abilities, small rooms and community spaces, not corporate offices.
- Avoid stock clichés: handshakes, hands in a circle, people pointing at laptops, heart-in-hands.
- Crop with breathing room. Photos get `radius-lg` corners on screen.

## Illustration

- Built from the same vocabulary as the marks: flat shapes, rounded corners, two colors per product plus `ink` and paper tints. No gradients, no outlines, no 3D.
- People are simple and faceless (heads as circles, bodies as rounded shapes), so the drawing stays friendly and never caricatures anyone.
- Use the product's accent once per illustration, on the thing that matters.

## Data visualization

- Series colors come from the product's primary first, then its accent, then `ink-soft`. For more series, use opacity steps of the primary.
- Good, warning and bad use the shared status colors and always carry a label.
- Axes and gridlines in `rule`; labels in `ink-soft` `caption`.
- Nonprofit Assessment's diverging bars (above and below a baseline) are the house style for comparing two groups.

## Social, slides and documents

- Background `paper` or `ink`. Headline in Fraunces 600, body in DM Sans.
- One product per image; its mark top-left or bottom-left with clear space; the endorsement line at the bottom on product images.
- Social images: 1200 × 630 for link previews, 1080 × 1080 for posts. Keep text under 12 words.
- Slides: 16:9, `paper` ground, one idea per slide, product color for the single key number or phrase.

## Favicons and app icons

- Favicon: the mark alone, at 32px and 16px, on transparent background.
- App icon: the reversed mark centered on a `<key>-primary` square with `radius-sm` corners scaled to the size (22% of the side).
